### WEEK 1, POST 1 — M365 misconfiguration (Educational)

Your M365 tenant is probably misconfigured right now.

Not maybe. Probably.

The default settings Microsoft ships are not secure settings. They are compatibility settings. They are built to make adoption easy, not to protect your data.

Here is what we find in almost every new M365 tenant we review:

Legacy authentication protocols still enabled. This is the single most common ransomware entry point in SMB environments. Legacy auth bypasses MFA entirely. Most tenants leave it on because turning it off sometimes breaks an old printer or shared mailbox nobody checked.

Conditional Access policies: absent or misconfigured. No MFA enforcement. No location-based restrictions. No device compliance requirements. The front door is open.

Global Admin accounts used for daily work. This is the equivalent of running your whole company as a Windows local admin. One phishing email and an attacker has full tenant control.

External sharing set to "anyone with the link." SharePoint and OneDrive default to permissive sharing. Your proposals, client files, and internal documents can be forwarded outside your organization with zero friction.

Audit logging disabled or retention set to 90 days. When something goes wrong — and eventually something does — you want 180 days minimum. 90 days means many incidents go undetected until the logs are already gone.

None of this requires advanced configuration. It requires someone to actually go through the tenant, check every setting against a baseline, and fix what Microsoft left open.

That is a standard part of every Klaravex onboarding.

If you have never had an M365 security audit, your tenant almost certainly has at least three of these issues open right now.

Worth a look.

### WEEK 2, POST 1 — Cyber insurance questionnaire (Educational)

Why most SMBs fail cyber insurance questionnaires the first time.

The questionnaire comes back. They answer honestly. The premium is three times what they expected, or coverage is declined outright.

The most common reasons:

No MFA on email. This is question one on nearly every cyber insurance questionnaire now. If you cannot say yes, your premium goes up significantly.

No endpoint detection and response (EDR). Antivirus is not EDR. "We have Windows Defender" is not the right answer on a 2026 questionnaire. Insurers want behavioral detection and response capability on every managed endpoint.

No formal incident response plan. Not a written document in a folder nobody reads — an actual plan that has been reviewed. Most SMBs do not have this.

No security awareness training with documented completion records. One phishing test per year does not count. Insurers want training records showing regular, documented participation.

Unsupported software in production. Windows 10 reaches end-of-life October 2025. End-of-life operating systems trigger exclusions.

Addressing all of this before you apply is almost always cheaper than paying the premium increase.

The questionnaire is a checklist. Work the checklist.

### WEEK 2, POST 2 — MDR: do you need it?

A question we hear regularly: "Do we really need an MDR, or is this just something MSPs sell?"

Honest answer: it depends on what you are protecting.

If you are a solo operator with five computers and no regulated data, basic managed antivirus and good M365 hygiene may be sufficient.

If you are a practice with patient records, a law firm with client files under attorney-client privilege, or a financial services firm handling client accounts, you need more than antivirus. You need detection — not just prevention.

Here is the distinction. Prevention tools try to block attacks before they succeed. Detection tools assume something will eventually get through and watch for it.

Ransomware groups know your tools. They deliberately move slowly to stay under the threshold that triggers simple pattern-matching alerts. MDR watches behavior over time, not just file signatures.

The cost of MDR for a 50-person practice is a fraction of what a single ransomware incident costs in downtime, recovery, and regulatory exposure under HIPAA.

Not selling anxiety. Selling a real cost-benefit calculation.

### WEEK 3, POST 1 — Network architecture (Educational)

Your firewall is not the problem. Your network architecture is.

Most SMBs have a single flat network. Everything on the same subnet. Your computers, your NAS, your IP phones, your guest Wi-Fi, your security cameras, your printer — all accessible to everything else.

Attackers who get into one device get into everything.

A guest Wi-Fi that shares a subnet with your file server is not guest Wi-Fi — it is a pivot point. A compromised laptop on a flat network can scan and hit your NAS, your internal servers, and every other device without crossing a single firewall rule.

Network segmentation is the fix. VLAN separation. Proper firewall rules between segments. Guest traffic isolated. IoT devices isolated. Server traffic isolated from workstations.

Ubiquiti UniFi makes this accessible at SMB price points. A properly configured UniFi deployment gives you VLAN segmentation, traffic inspection, and centralized management at a fraction of what this cost five years ago.

Every Klaravex deployment includes UniFi management and network segmentation as standard. Not as an add-on. Part of the foundation.

If you have a flat network right now, that is the first conversation worth having.

### WEEK 3, POST 2 — M365 migration reality check

A question fielded recently: "We are moving from G Suite to M365 — how complicated is this actually?"

Direct answer: more complicated than Microsoft's migration guide suggests, and less complicated than most people fear, if you plan it properly.

Here is what actually takes time.

Mail coexistence. If you are running both platforms during a phased migration, you need MX records, routing rules, and shared calendar visibility working correctly. "Just change the MX record" results in lost email.

SharePoint/Google Drive parity. The folder structure in Google Drive does not map 1:1 to SharePoint. The migration tool matters, and the folder architecture decision matters more.

License timing. You will pay for both platforms during transition. Rushing it causes issues. Dragging it out costs money.

Admin tenant configuration. M365 needs to be configured properly before users land on it — Conditional Access, MFA, external sharing policies, DLP rules. Landing users on a default M365 tenant is landing them on an unsecured one.

Realistic timeline for a 50-person migration done properly: four to six weeks from kickoff to fully decommissioned Google Workspace.

If you are planning a migration in the next quarter, start the scoping conversation now. The complexity is manageable. The mistakes are all predictable.

### WEEK 4, POST 1 — HIPAA clarification (Educational)

HIPAA is not a technology standard. It is a risk management standard.

There is no HIPAA-certified firewall. There is no HIPAA-compliant operating system. What HIPAA requires is that you identify risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of PHI — and address them with reasonable and appropriate safeguards.

"Reasonable and appropriate" scales to your size and budget. A 10-person practice has different obligations than a 500-person hospital.

What this means in practice:

You need a Security Risk Analysis. Written. Documented. Updated when your environment changes. This is the foundation of HIPAA Security Rule compliance. Most small practices either have not done one or have a document that is five years old.

You need documented policies. Access control, workforce training, incident response, device management.

You need technical safeguards that match your documented policies. If your policy says MFA is required for PHI systems, MFA needs to be on.

The enforcement pattern for small practices is almost always: breach occurs, OCR investigates, no SRA on file, six-figure settlement.

The work to get ahead of this is not enormous. The cost of not doing it can be existential.

If you are a healthcare-adjacent practice that has never done a formal Security Risk Analysis, that is the starting point for any conversation with us.
