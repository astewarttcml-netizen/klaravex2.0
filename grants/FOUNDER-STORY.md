## Long version (~250 words — essay fields)

I've spent more than fifteen years in enterprise IT — building, securing, and running
the systems businesses live on. For most of that career I was the only Black engineer
in the room, and often the only gay one too. **[ADD: one concrete moment that shaped
you — a passed-over promotion, a client who assumed you were junior, the day you
decided to go independent.]** Eventually I stopped waiting for a seat at the table and
built my own: an independent consultancy in Berlin, where I served small businesses as
their senior engineer for everything from Microsoft 365 and Azure to security audits.

What I saw there became Klaravex. Small businesses get the worst of IT: enterprise
problems, consumer budgets, and providers who treat them as an afterthought. So I
built an AI-native managed services provider — 78 production AI agents I engineered
myself handle first-line support, triage, and compliance workflows around the clock,
and a senior engineer (me) owns every outcome. AI does the heavy lifting; a human is
always accountable. That model lets a one-person, Black- and LGBTQ+-owned company
deliver what used to take a ten-person MSP — at prices a five-person business can
actually afford.

Klaravex LLC is bootstrapped — registered in Wyoming, run from my base in Los Angeles. I'm building the company I needed to exist when I
was coming up: technically excellent, radically transparent, and owned by someone who
looks like me.
