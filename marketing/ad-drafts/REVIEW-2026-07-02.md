## 🔴 Must-fix before launch

### 1. Google RSA headline over 30 chars (Ad Group 1, headline 15)

`Small Practice, Enterprise Care` = **31 chars**, Google will reject.

Fix: `Small Practice, Enterprise IT` (29) or `Small Firms, Big-Firm Security` (30).

### 2. "Audit-Ready in 90 Days" as a headline (RSAs Ad Groups 1 + 4)

Google requires substantiation for outcome claims. Regulatory audit-readiness in a fixed timeframe is a hard promise a reasonable regulator or auditor could dispute if the client's baseline is worse than assumed.

Fix: soften to `Readiness in ~90 Days` (22) or `Fast-Track Audit Readiness` (25) or drop this claim.

### 3. "SEC/FINRA-Ready IT Security" (RSA Ad Group 3, headline 4)

FINRA doesn't regulate accountants. SEC only regulates firms it registers (investment advisers, broker-dealers). If the ad group targets general "accountants and RIAs," conflate carefully — RIAs are SEC-regulated but pure CPAs aren't.

Fix: `SEC-Ready IT for RIAs` (21) as its own ad group. Keep FTC Safeguards for the CPA-general audience.

### 4. Ad Group 6 brand mixes "AI" claim with "regulated SMBs trust Klaravex" (headline 9)

"Regulated SMBs Trust Klaravex" implies existing customer trust. If Klaravex is early-stage without volume clients yet, this is aspirational. Google's editorial team increasingly flags social-proof claims without evidence.

Fix: `Built for Regulated SMBs` (23) — states positioning, not claimed trust.

## 🟡 Soft flags — reviewer opinion, take or leave

### 5. Character-count labels in the doc are sometimes off by 1

Not a Google issue (Google counts actual chars). But if Anthony pastes into Google Ads and the character counter doesn't match what the doc claims, could cause a "why does this say 30 when it's 29?" moment.

The following headlines have labels that don't match actual character count (all still under limit — safe to paste, but relabel if the doc is going to another human):

- Ad Group 1: HL 1 (claimed 22, actual 23), HL 3 (29 → 28), HL 11 (28 → 27)
- Ad Group 2: HL 1 (26 → 25), HL 11 (26 → 27)
- Ad Group 4: HL 2 (22 → 21), HL 7 (23 → 22)
- Ad Group 5: HL 6 (22 → 21), HL 7 (23 → 24), HL 9 (28 → 27), HL 14 (28 → 27)
- Ad Group 6: HL 8 (23 → 22), HL 12 (26 → 27)

Cosmetic. Fix later.

### 6. "Anthony" appears 4× in the drafts

Confirmed clean — all 4 are operational references (Sender of LinkedIn Conversation Ad, launch-checklist notes to Anthony, etc.), not visible in ad copy.

BUT — for the LinkedIn Conversation Ad (T-AC-17), the sender is Anthony Stewart personally. Brand strategy says voice is "third-person corporate." Two options:
- (a) Send as `Klaravex — Managing Director` (title-only, minimizes personal-name association)
- (b) Accept the Anthony-as-sender because Conversation Ads have higher engagement when they feel personal

Recommend (b) — LinkedIn Conversation Ads systematically underperform when sent from an anonymous role. The personal-brand tradeoff is worth it. But flag it as an exception to the "no personal names" voice rule.

### 7. T-AC-16 lead magnet — 12 domains × 4-6 checklist items = 60-72 items to score

The self-score guidance says "40-48 checkmarks: audit-ready." That's inconsistent with a 60-72 item range. If a domain has 6 items and there are 12 domains, max 72 checks, so the thresholds should scale (e.g. `65-72 audit-ready · 50-64 real program with gaps · 35-49 tools without program · 0-34 exposure`).

Fix: tighten Page 10 thresholds to match actual item count once the checklist is finalized (may need to trim to exactly 4 items per domain = 48 max, keeping the doc's stated math).

### 8. T-AC-06 conversion values are estimates

`readiness_review_booked = $250`, `directive_quote_requested = $500` are placeholders labeled "rough estimates." Google Ads Smart Bidding is very sensitive to these — if the values are 3× too high, Google over-invests in low-quality clicks; too low and it under-invests in high-quality ones.

Fix: revisit these numbers after 30 days of actual pipeline data. Directive tier LTV is likely $10-30K annualized; a booked review that converts 10-20% = $1K-$6K expected value → set at $1,500 initially. Readiness review has lower conversion; $250 is defensible.

## Cross-cutting recommendations

### A. Add a single "landing page requirements" doc referenced by all four ads

Every ad group in T-AC-11 says "landing page: klaravex.com/xxxx (build if missing)." Currently no single owner for those pages. Recommend:

1. Create `marketing/ad-drafts/landing-pages-required.md` listing every URL referenced across T-AC-11/-16/-17
2. Cross-reference each vertical landing page spec to `marketing/vciso-service-pages-US-draft.md` (which already has healthcare/legal/financial vertical drafts partially built)
3. Tag pages as **live | draft | not-started** so the launch checklist has a clear go/no-go

### B. Add a suppression list to Google Ads and LinkedIn

Both platforms need explicit exclusions:
- **Google Ads** — negative keyword lists for "CMMC," "DIB," "defense contractor," "cannabis," "ITAR," "sports betting," "porn" (unlikely searches but Google's smart bidding can drift)
- **LinkedIn** — exclude Public Sector, Aerospace & Defense, Casino & Gambling, Cannabis, Adult Entertainment functions from all audiences

### C. Add a monitoring / kill-switch rule

If any ad group's CPA > 3× target for 3 consecutive days AND >20 conversions in that window, auto-pause. Set this as a Google Ads automated rule + a manual weekly check in T-AC-18.

### D. Regulatory language — request compliance-adjacent counsel review

The word "audit-ready" and specific regulator names (FTC, SEC, HIPAA, FINRA) are ad-industry-restricted. Google and LinkedIn both have restricted-category verification (T-AC-02). If Klaravex claims aren't verified through those categories, ads will run for a few days then get flagged.

Not a "you got the copy wrong" — Loki isn't sued. This is a "add T-AC-02 to the critical path before running these" reminder.
