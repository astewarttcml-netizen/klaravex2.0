# Competitive Brief — Klaravex US (klaravex.com)

**Scope:** US side only (klaravex.com). Healthcare / legal / financial SMB managed IT + managed security + compliance *readiness* (HIPAA, SOC 2, ISO 27001), MDR, vCISO.
**Excluded:** EU / klaravex.com (NIS2, GDPR, German Mittelstand) — separate analysis per the no-vertical-mixing rule.
**Research method:** Public web research (homepages, pricing pages, press, G2/Capterra/TrustRadius/Glassdoor, SERP scan). Ahrefs/Similarweb **not connected** — no quantitative traffic/backlink/keyword-volume data in this version; see "Data Limitations."
**Research date:** 2026-06-23. Treat all competitor facts as of this date.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The US SMB compliance-and-security market splits into three camps, and **none of them occupy Klaravex's exact intended position** — a directly-sold managed service that fuses compliance *readiness advisory* + *MDR* + *vCISO* for healthcare, legal, and financial SMBs.

- **Platforms that sell software, not service** (Coro, Cynomi, Compliancy Group, Electric): they hand the customer a tool and leave the human delivery, audit-readiness, and incident response to someone else — or to an MSP partner. Cynomi is explicitly 100% channel-only and *needs* an MSP like Klaravex to reach the SMB.
- **Full-spectrum MSPs where compliance is buried** (Ntiva): compliance is one service line among twenty, and their strongest compliance vertical is CMMC/GovCon — which Klaravex has deliberately excluded.
- **Vertical/keyword gaps nobody owns:** ISO 27001 for US SMBs, vertical-specific vCISO pages (law firms, medical practices), FTC Safeguards Rule for accounting firms, and 2026 HHS Security Rule update content are all uncontested or thin.

**Biggest opportunity:** Own the "service layer the platforms are missing" — buyer-facing, vertical-specific readiness content and a directly-sold Directive-tier offering (readiness + MDR + vCISO) that Coro, Cynomi, and Compliancy Group structurally cannot deliver.

**Biggest threat:** Capital and brand velocity. Coro ($252M raised) and Cynomi ($61M, 3x ARR, 300+ MSP partners) are outspending the entire category on content and can flood the SEO space faster than a lean operator. If Klaravex competes on generic "SMB cybersecurity" terms, it loses on domain authority. It must win on narrow, vertical, regulation-specific intent where the funded players have no incentive to go deep.

## 3. Competitor Profiles

### 3.1 Ntiva — the scaled, PE-backed full-spectrum MSP

| | |
|---|---|
| Positioning | "Managed IT Services Built Around Your Business" — local pods + national 24/7 desk |
| Target | SMB / mid-market; broad vertical spread |
| Scale | ~500–700 employees; PE-backed (PSP Partners, 2022); 14 acquisitions; MSP 501 #11 (2025) |
| Compliance | CMMC Level 2 certified (own ops), SOC 2 Type 1 (own ops); HIPAA + SEC/FINRA named |
| Pricing | Opaque — "Signature Support Plans," sales-gated, no public rates |

**Messaging themes:** proactive-not-reactive; local + national; strategic partner not vendor; security/compliance "built in from day one"; transparency.

**Strengths:** genuine scale and certifications (CMMC L2 is rare at SMB-MSP level); vertical landing pages capturing organic search; active content + webinar engine; June 2026 blog signals they're formalizing a GRC service line.

**Weaknesses / attack surface:**
- Compliance is **buried**, not the lead — a compliance-first buyer may not identify Ntiva as the answer.
- **ISO 27001 absent** from prominent messaging.
- CMMC/GovCon dominance creates a "this is for government contractors, not my clinic" perception.
- Glassdoor 2.5/5 (155 reviews) — internal stress typical of PE rollups under cost pressure; service-quality risk.
- Only ~1 verified G2 review — thin third-party proof for their scale.

**How Klaravex wins:** "Your MSP appends compliance. We lead with it. Your vCISO doesn't manage your endpoints; your endpoint vendor doesn't sit in your board meeting. We do both."

### 3.2 Electric (electric.ai) — the VC-backed "MSP alternative" platform

| | |
|---|---|
| Positioning | "One platform for all your IT and Security needs" — software, "no tech skills required" |
| Target | Generic SMB; ops managers, not IT staff |
| Scale | $211M raised (Series D); NYC; multiple layoff rounds (2022–2024); ~3.7/5 Capterra (23 reviews) |
| Compliance | **Effectively none** — no HIPAA, SOC 2, ISO 27001, or vCISO offering |
| Pricing | Public & low: $10/user/mo Essentials, $25 Pro; security add-ons extra |

**Strengths:** transparent low pricing; "MSP Alternative" framing; HRIS integration (Rippling/Justworks) for onboarding automation; recognizable brand.

**Weaknesses / attack surface:**
- **No compliance advisory, no vCISO, no on-site support.** A healthcare/legal/fintech Electric customer hits a wall the moment a HIPAA/SOC 2 requirement lands.
- "All-in-one" is partly resold third-party tools (reviewers complain of tool sprawl underneath).
- Layoff-driven service-delivery complaints; pivot toward embedding in HR platforms suggests retreat from direct SMB sales.

**How Klaravex wins:** Electric is a **stepping stone**. "Electric provisions laptops. It can't make you HIPAA-ready, prep a SOC 2 audit, or put a vCISO in front of your board." Klaravex is the upgrade path for regulated SMBs that outgrow it.

### 3.3 Coro (coro.net) — the funded SMB cybersecurity *platform*

| | |
|---|---|
| Positioning | "Workspace security built for Lean IT teams" — one platform, one dashboard, one AI agent |
| Target | SMB 25–500 users; "Lean IT" persona; sold **exclusively through MSPs/resellers** |
| Scale | $252M raised; $750M valuation (Series D, 2024); 3,000+ customers; G2 Leader badges |
| Compliance | **Shallow** — `/compliance` + HIPAA page map modules to controls; compliance-as-byproduct |
| Pricing | Now partner-quoted (was ~$9.50/user/mo); year-2 price jumps flagged in reviews |

**Strengths:** real platform consolidation (single agent across endpoint/email/cloud/network/data); SE Labs AAA; strong G2 momentum; sophisticated "Lean IT Day" category-building; channel economics that motivate MSPs.

**Weaknesses / attack surface:**
- **No advisory, no readiness, no audit prep, no vCISO, no MDR** delivered by Coro itself — it routes managed services to partners.
- False-positive / alert-fatigue complaints; "limited response actions" undercut the 92% auto-resolution claim.
- A Coro customer who must actually *pass* a HIPAA or SOC 2 audit still needs a human advisor — Klaravex's lane.

**How Klaravex wins:** Coro is a tool Klaravex could *deploy*, not a competitor for the service. Position as "the readiness + MDR + vCISO layer that turns security tooling into an audit-ready posture."

### 3.4 Compliancy Group (compliancy-group.com) — the HIPAA compliance *software* incumbent

| | |
|---|---|
| Positioning | "Healthcare Compliance Simplified" — G2 #1 rated healthcare compliance software ("The Guard") |
| Target | Small healthcare practices, dental (ADA-endorsed), healthcare vendors/BAs |
| Scale | Founded 2005; ~70 employees; $75M PE (Aldrich, 2022); **acquired Healthicity June 17, 2026** (3,000+ orgs combined) |
| Compliance | Deep documentation/training/workflow; **no security technology underneath** |
| Pricing | Public: Foundation $99/mo → Elite $449+/mo, +$8–10/employee; multi-framework add-on $299/mo |

**Strengths:** category ownership in HIPAA software; ADA sole endorsement; "HIPAA Seal of Compliance" trust badge; huge evergreen SEO footprint (free checklist, free training, HIPAA fines directory); transparent pricing; 20-year track record; new multi-framework crosswalk (HIPAA+SOC 2+ISO 27001+NIST+CIS).

**Weaknesses / attack surface:**
- **No cybersecurity controls, no MDR, no vCISO, no incident response.** It's documentation + training. A breach hits and The Guard can't contain it.
- The "Seal" is **self-attestation**, not a third-party audit — Klaravex can position real gap assessment vs. software checkboxes.
- Reviewer churn risk: PE-driven shift from high-touch coaching to self-serve has alienated long-tenured customers; onboarding-fee and portal-complexity complaints.
- Healthcare-locked; doesn't extend cleanly to legal/financial.

**How Klaravex wins:** "Compliancy Group documents your program. It doesn't secure your endpoints, watch your network, or answer the phone at 2am during a breach. Readiness is a service, not a seal."

### 3.5 Cynomi (cynomi.com) — the vCISO platform (channel-only; content competitor, not sales competitor)

| | |
|---|---|
| Positioning | "The Security Growth Platform for Service Providers" (rebranded from "vCISO platform") |
| Target | **MSPs/MSSPs/consultancies only — 100% channel, never sells to SMBs** |
| Scale | $61M raised; Series B $37M (Apr 2025); 3x ARR 2024; 300+ partners; on Pax8; G2 4.9/5 |
| Compliance | 40+ frameworks in one assessment; compliance framed as an *outcome* of a security program |
| Pricing | Opaque — custom; packaging restructure underway (CEO note, June 2026) |

**Why it matters to Klaravex:** Cynomi is **not** a direct sales competitor — it's (a) a potential *tool* Klaravex could run under its own Directive-tier delivery, and (b) a well-funded **content/SEO competitor** crowding the compliance + vCISO keyword space.

**Strengths:** category clarity; framework breadth; Revenue-Intelligence module (maps gaps → MSP services); aggressive content (3–5 posts/week, framework landing pages, vCISO Academy); AI "CISO Intelligence" agents (2026).

**Weaknesses / blind spots Klaravex exploits:**
- Writes **to MSP operators, not to SMB buyers** — no content explaining HIPAA to a dental office or SOC 2 to a boutique law firm. That buyer-facing layer is open.
- No vertical-specific service content — everything is horizontal MSP ops.
- Session-centric architecture (no cross-client portfolio view) — relevant only if Klaravex evaluates it as a tool.

**How Klaravex wins:** Don't fight Cynomi on MSP-ops content. Own the **SMB-buyer-facing, vertical, regulation-specific** content Cynomi has no reason to write — and consider Cynomi as the delivery backbone behind the Directive tier.

**Themes Klaravex can own outright (low competition + on-strategy + regulation-timely):**
1. Proposed 2026 HHS HIPAA Security Rule update (NPRM) — what's coming and how small practices should prepare.
2. FTC Safeguards Rule readiness for accounting/CPA firms.
3. Vertical vCISO service pages (law firms, medical practices, financial advisors).
4. US ISO 27001 readiness for professional-services SMBs.
5. SOC 2 + HIPAA dual readiness for health-tech startups.

## 7. Opportunities

1. **Own the "delivered, not DIY" frame.** Every funded competitor ships a tool and offloads delivery. Klaravex's single accountable readiness + MDR + vCISO service is the position none of them hold. Make that the homepage thesis.
2. **Vertical depth as moat.** Cynomi, Coro, Electric are all horizontal. Compliancy Group is healthcare-only software. Vertical service pages + vertical content (law, medical, financial) are uncontested and match Klaravex's ICP exactly.
3. **Regulation-timeliness as a content wedge.** The 2026 HHS Security Rule update and FTC Safeguards Rule are fresh, high-intent, and unclaimed. Lean operators can move faster on timely content than PE/VC content machines bound to editorial calendars.
4. **Partner-channel arbitrage.** Cynomi (and Coro) explicitly need MSPs. Klaravex can run Cynomi/Coro under the hood to deliver Directive-tier outcomes while keeping the brand and the client relationship — buying capability without buying headcount.
5. **Trust-signal gap.** Ntiva (1 G2 review) and the platforms rely on their own seals. Early, real third-party reviews (G2, Clutch) plus actual third-party gap assessments differentiate against self-attestation.

---

## 8. Threats

1. **Capital and content velocity.** Coro ($252M) and Cynomi ($61M, 3–5 posts/week, framework landing pages) can dominate broad SEO. Competing on generic "SMB cybersecurity" loses. Mitigation: narrow, vertical, regulation-specific intent only.
2. **Ntiva formalizing GRC.** June 2026 GRC content signals they're moving toward a compliance service line — directly into Klaravex's lane, with far more scale. Mitigation: out-specialize on healthcare/legal/financial verticals and ISO 27001 before they generalize into it.
3. **Compliancy Group + Healthicity (June 2026).** A bigger healthcare-compliance platform with 3,000+ orgs strengthens the incumbent in Klaravex's #1 vertical. Mitigation: attack the "software ≠ security, seal ≠ audit" gap; lead with MDR + vCISO they can't offer.
4. **Price anchoring.** Electric ($10–25/user) and third-party vCISO listicles ($1,600–5,000/mo) set buyer expectations. Mitigation: never compete on Foundation price (per GTM rule); anchor on Directive-tier outcomes and risk-avoidance, not per-seat cost.
5. **Platform "good enough" perception.** Buyers may believe Coro/Compliancy Group alone = compliant. Mitigation: content that explicitly shows the gap between tooling/documentation and audit-ready, actively-defended posture.

## 9. Recommended Actions

**Quick wins (this week / this month)**
1. Publish two uncontested, regulation-timely cornerstone pieces: **"The Proposed 2026 HIPAA Security Rule Update: What Small Medical Practices Should Prepare For"** (frame as *proposed/coming*, not in-effect — it's an NPRM, not final) and **"FTC Safeguards Rule Readiness for Accounting Firms"** (already in effect — uncontested SERP). Both align to ICP.
2. Stand up **vertical vCISO service pages** — "vCISO for small law firms," "vCISO for medical practices," "vCISO for financial advisory firms." Purchase-ready intent, near-zero competition.
3. Ship a **free interactive readiness self-assessment** (HIPAA / SOC 2 / ISO 27001) as a lead-gen tool — the healthcare-SMB SERP has none, while Compliancy Group and Cynomi both monetize this format.
4. Seed **third-party reviews** (G2, Clutch) early — the incumbents are thin or self-attested; this is cheap differentiation.

**Strategic moves (next 1–2 quarters)**
5. Make the homepage thesis **"compliance readiness, delivered — not a tool you're left to run alone,"** leading with the Directive tier (readiness + MDR + vCISO). This is the open quadrant; claim it explicitly.
6. Decide the **build-vs-buy on Cynomi/Coro** as the delivery backbone for Directive — capability without headcount, while retaining brand and client ownership.
7. Build the **"US ISO 27001 readiness for professional-services SMBs"** content cluster — no US MSP ranks, and the EU-side knowledge gives Klaravex a credible US/EU bridge no competitor has.
8. Establish a **monthly competitive monitor** on Ntiva's GRC build-out, Compliancy Group/Healthicity integration, and Coro/Cynomi content cadence — and a quarterly deep refresh of this brief.
