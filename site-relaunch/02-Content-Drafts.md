### D4. /personal/scam-recovery/  (FREE — DO NOT PUBLISH until E&O bound + counsel review)
**H1:** Targeted by a romance or online scam? Take a breath. Here's what to do — and we'll help, free.

If you've been caught in a romance scam or online fraud, the most important thing first: **this is not your fault, and you are not alone.** These operations are professional, organized, and designed to fool smart people. Acting quickly in the right order protects you from further harm.

**Do these things now (and we'll walk you through any of them, free):**
1. **Stop all contact** with the scammer. Don't warn them, don't explain — just stop.
2. **Contact your bank or card company immediately.** Speed matters: debit/Zelle have a short window for the strongest protection; most credit cards can dispute charges.
3. **Secure your accounts and devices.** Scammers often install hidden remote-access software. Change passwords from a *different, clean* device and turn on two-factor authentication.
4. **Report it** — it builds the case against them:
   - FTC: **reportfraud.ftc.gov**
   - FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center: **ic3.gov**
   - If it involved crypto or "investments": also the SEC (sec.gov/tcr)
   - Local police (for a report number your bank may need)
5. **Save everything** — messages, profiles, receipts, wallet addresses.

**Free, confidential support — call the AARP Fraud Watch Helpline at 877-908-3360** (open to everyone, not just AARP members). It's run by people trained to help fraud victims, including emotional support.

### How Klaravex helps — for free
We're a technology and security firm, so here's what we'll do at no charge:
- **Secure your devices** — find and remove hidden remote-access software, clean malware, lock things down.
- **Protect your accounts** — reset passwords safely, turn on two-factor, and revoke any access the scammer gained.
- **Help you organize your evidence** into a clear package you can submit to the FTC, IC3, and your bank.
- **Explain your options in plain English** and point you to the right licensed professionals.

### ⚠️ Beware the *second* scam — the "recovery" scam
After a scam, criminals sell your details to other criminals who pose as "recovery services," law firms, or even cybersecurity teams promising to get your money back. **It is often the same people.** Protect yourself:
- **Never pay an upfront fee to recover money.** No legitimate service — and no government agency — charges you to get your funds back. This single rule defeats almost every recovery scam.
- Be suspicious of *anyone who contacts you first* offering to recover funds.
- Watch for escalating "tax," "insurance," or "activation" fees — that's the trap.

- We provide **technical assistance only** — we are **not** attorneys, private investigators, or financial advisors. Nothing here is legal, financial, or investigative advice.
- We **do not guarantee that any money can be recovered.** No honest service can.
- We **do not file reports for you** without your explicit written direction, and we **never charge a fee to recover funds.**
