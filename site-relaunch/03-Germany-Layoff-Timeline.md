## ⚠️ TIME-SENSITIVE — READ FIRST
Under German law, an employee who wants to challenge a dismissal generally must file a *Kündigungsschutzklage* (protection-against-dismissal claim) at the **Arbeitsgericht (labor court) within 3 weeks (21 calendar days) of receiving the written termination.** Miss it and the dismissal is usually deemed valid, regardless of merit.

> **Why it matters (cross-border point):** A US parent with German offices does **not** convert a German job into US "at-will." If you worked in Germany under the German entity, German employment law very likely governs — strong dismissal protection, statutory notice, mandatory written form — regardless of the parent's nationality. Even a US choice-of-law clause generally **cannot strip mandatory German protections** for work performed in Germany. The likely scenario to test with counsel: a US company applied a US severance reflex ("flight home, done") to an employee who was actually protected by German law. The refusal to cover expenses may be a contractual/statutory shortfall, not merely a courtesy declined.
