# Focused Go-To-Market — Consumer Line
**One market, one or two channels, first. Beat surface-area with depth.**

**Status:** Strategy draft for review.
**Scope:** The consumer line (personal.klaravex.*). The B2B flagship (klaravex.com, US) is a separate motion and is **not** what this plan addresses.

---

## The decision: pick a beachhead, don't split four ways

You have four possible consumer fronts: DE-remote, DE-local, US-remote, US-local. As a solo operator (even with agents), launching all four dilutes everything — ad budget, reviews, SEO authority, and your attention. **Win one, then expand from strength.**

### Recommended beachhead: **Berlin / Germany, remote-first consumer**

| Front | Demand intent | Your fit | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DE consumer (Berlin/remote)** | High — `Computerhilfe`/`Fernwartung` actively searched on Kleinanzeigen | You're physically present; established local credibility; German-language vehicle exists | **Beachhead** |
| US consumer (remote) | Moderate but **saturated** — every remote helpdesk competes | Remote-only; no local trust anchor; timezone-distant | Defer to Phase 2 |
| DE/US local in-person | Real but unscalable for a solo operator | Berlin in-person possible; US impossible | Berlin in-person only as a premium add-on later |

Rationale: demand intent + your physical presence + an existing German footprint align in **one** place. US consumer is a worse market entered from a weaker position — enter it later, remotely, once the model and reviews are proven.

---

## ICP for the beachhead

- **Primary:** Berlin-area individuals and households, English- or German-speaking, frustrated with everyday tech (slow PC, Wi-Fi, accounts, privacy), who value *patient, plain-language* help and *instant 24/7 response*.
- **Secondary:** Expats in Germany underserved by German-only support — a segment you're uniquely positioned for (English-first).
- **Highest-LTV:** Parents/seniors and solo entrepreneurs — recurring needs, subscription-friendly, refer heavily.

## Offer (from the corrected pricing model)

- **On-ramp:** AI Chat €/$ low tier (agent-only, 24/7) — the "anyone can afford help" entry, per your "smaller guys" intent.
- **Core:** Essential €59-equiv/mo, human-backed — the tier to push.
- **Founding rate:** temporary discounted Essential for first 50 clients — how you "pass savings" now without anchoring low.

## Channel focus — TWO channels, not six

| Priority | Channel | Why | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#1** | **Kleinanzeigen.de** (`Computerhilfe / Fernwartung`) | Where German consumers literally search for this. Highest-intent, lowest-cost. | Free + small paid bump |
| **#2** | **Google Business Profile (SAB)** | Map-pack for "Computerhilfe Berlin"; reviews compound. | Free |
| Defer | Thumbtack, MyHammer, Nebenan, paid ads | Add only after #1/#2 produce booked jobs and reviews | — |

**Do not open channel #3 until #1 and #2 are producing.** Concentration is the strategy.

## 30 / 60 / 90 plan

**Days 1–30 — foundation + first cases**
- GBP (service-area) live: category "Computer support," 24/7 hours, Phase-1 AI-disclosure-compliant description.
- Kleinanzeigen listing live with AI-assist disclosure in the ad text (not just the site).
- Seed 5–10 genuine reviews from first customers — reviews are the entire consumer-IT ballgame.
- Start the case log day one (feeds graduation + pricing).

**Days 31–60 — prove unit economics**
- Hit ~30–50 cases. Watch fixed-cost/case fall in the pricing model.
- Begin graduating the first Green category (password resets / Wi-Fi) per the tracker.
- Convert one-time customers → Essential subscription. Track conversion rate.

**Days 61–90 — tighten and decide**
- Review: cost-per-acquired-customer per channel, subscription conversion %, churn, first graduated category.
- Kill anything above breakeven CPA. Double down on what books jobs.
- Decision gate: only now consider channel #3 or the US-remote front.

---

## Metrics that matter

| Metric | Target (90 days) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Booked cases / mo | ≥ 50 | Drives fixed-cost/case down (the model's key lever) |
| Cost per acquired customer | < first-month margin of Essential | Channel must pay for itself |
| One-time → subscription conversion | ≥ 25% | Recurring revenue is the real asset |
| Reviews (GBP + Kleinanzeigen) | ≥ 15 | Conversion multiplier for consumer |
| First Green category graduated | 1 | Proves the automation flywheel turns |

## Risks

- **Spreading too early** (the main one) — resist opening more channels/markets until the beachhead pays.
- **Review cold-start** — no reviews = low conversion. Manually solicit every early happy customer.
- **Disclosure-in-ad gap** — platforms review the listing, not your site; the AI-assist line must be in the ad copy itself.
- **Compliance gate** — Impressum/DSGVO must be live before German ads (see compliance block).

## The one-line strategy
Win Berlin consumer on Kleinanzeigen + GBP, convert to subscriptions, graduate one automation category, *then* expand. Depth first.
