## SECTION 2: THE REGULATORY CONTEXT

**Headline:** NIS2 and DORA — what they require

**NIS2 Directive**
The NIS2 Directive was adopted in December 2022. It replaces the original NIS Directive and significantly expands scope — covering operators in energy, transport, banking, health, digital infrastructure, and other essential and important entity categories. EU member states were required to transpose NIS2 into national law by October 2024; transposition timelines and national specifics vary.

Key requirements: risk management measures (including incident response, supply chain security, and business continuity), incident reporting to national competent authorities within defined timelines, and governance obligations at management body level — with personal liability for senior management.

**DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act)**
DORA applies to financial entities operating in the EU — banks, investment firms, insurance undertakings, crypto-asset service providers, and more — as defined in Article 2 of Regulation (EU) 2022/2554. DORA became applicable in January 2025.

Core requirements: an ICT risk management framework, ICT-related incident classification and reporting, digital operational resilience testing (including TLPT for significant entities), ICT third-party risk management, and information-sharing arrangements.

## SECTION 7: FAQ

**Q: How do we know if NIS2 applies to our organization?**
A: NIS2 scope depends on your sector and entity size — with "essential" and "important" entity categories carrying different obligations. National competent authorities in each member state may also have registration or notification requirements. We assist with technical scoping analysis, but formal legal determination requires qualified EU legal counsel.

**Q: DORA applies to financial entities — are SMEs covered?**
A: Yes, with some proportionality provisions. DORA applies to a broad range of financial entities regardless of size, though the intensity of requirements (e.g., digital operational resilience testing) scales with entity size and systemic importance. Small non-interconnected investment firms have a simplified regime.

**Q: We're based in Germany — does the national NIS2 law change what we need to do?**
A: Germany transposed NIS2 through the NIS2UmsuCG (NIS-2-Umsetzungs- und Cybersicherheitsstärkungsgesetz). The BSI is the competent authority for most entities. Requirements are broadly aligned with the Directive, with German-specific registration and reporting procedures. We work with German-based organizations regularly and understand the BSI context.
