## SECTION 2: THE REGULATORY CONTEXT

**ISO/IEC 27001:2022**
ISO 27001 is the international standard for information security management systems (ISMS). The 2022 revision introduced new controls and reorganized the Annex A control set. Certification is awarded by an accredited certification body following a Stage 1 (documentation review) and Stage 2 (operational audit).

For many organizations, ISO 27001 certification is a commercial requirement — enterprise customers and procurement teams ask for it. For others, the ISMS framework itself is the goal — a structured, documented approach to managing information security risk, regardless of formal certification.

Both paths require the same foundation: a defined scope, a documented risk treatment process, a set of operational policies and procedures, and ongoing management review.

**GDPR (EU and UK)**
The General Data Protection Regulation applies to any organization that processes personal data of EU or UK residents — regardless of where the organization is based. Key technical obligations include: appropriate technical and organizational measures (TOMs) to protect personal data, data mapping, lawful basis documentation, data subject rights processes (access, erasure, portability), breach notification within 72 hours, and DPA agreements with processors.

Many organizations operate under GDPR without a systematic approach to their technical obligations. A data breach followed by a supervisory authority investigation will surface exactly which controls were missing.

## SECTION 3: WHERE SMBS TYPICALLY FAIL

**Headline:** The gaps we see most often

**ISO 27001:**
- Scope not formally defined — the ISMS boundary is vague or inconsistently applied
- Risk assessment done once, never updated
- Controls selected without a documented risk treatment decision
- Statement of Applicability (SoA) either missing or a cut-and-paste from a template
- Management review done informally (or not at all)
- Internal audit not completed before Stage 2

**GDPR:**
- No data mapping or Record of Processing Activities (ROPA) — required under Article 30
- Lawful basis not documented for each processing activity
- Data subject requests handled ad hoc, without a process or SLA
- Processors not identified — no DPA agreements in place
- No breach notification procedure aligned to the 72-hour timeline
- Technical measures not documented — organizations can't demonstrate what they've done

A: Yes. ISO 27001 is not just for large enterprises. Scope definition is flexible — a well-scoped ISMS for a 20-person company is entirely achievable. The key is defining a realistic scope and building an ISMS that is operational, not just documented.
